    Worksheet 0. Review of Calculus III
    

    MTH 401

    Objectives

    It’s important we recall the following ideas which we will use throughout this class:
    🔗
    • The dot product and the cross product.
      🔗
      🔗
    • How to parameterize a curve.
      🔗
      🔗
    • The gradient of a function.
      🔗
      🔗
    • Fields.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    1.

    Consider the vectors
    \begin{align*} A \amp = (2,-3,2), \amp B \amp= (-1,0,3). \end{align*}
    🔗

    (a)

    Find the dot products \(A\cdot A\) and \(A\cdot B\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Answer.
    \begin{equation*} A\cdot A = 17, \ \ \ A\cdot B = 4 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{align*} A\cdot A \amp = 2^2 + (-3)^2+2^2 = 17\\ A\cdot B \amp =(2)(-1)+(-3)(0)+(2)(3) =4 \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    How does this help you find the length of \(A\text{?}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    What is the angle between \(A\) and \(B\text{?}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    (d)

    How can the dot product detect when two vectors are orthogonal?
    🔗
    🔗

    (e)

    If \(C\) is a vector and \(C\cdot C=0\text{,}\) what does this say about \(C\text{?}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    (f)

    Find the cross product \(A\times B\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    (g)

    Describe in words how you would find the direction in which \(A\times B\) points.
    🔗
    🔗

    (h)

    What is \(A\times A\text{?}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    Shown below are a line and a circle.
    🔗
    
    The graph of a line that goes through the points \((-1,2)\) and \((1,1)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    
    The graph of a circle with radius \(2\) centered at \((-1,-1)\text{.}\)
    🔗

    (a)

    Create a parametrization for each of the curves.
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    What choices have you made in forming your parametrizations and how might your parametrizations differ from a classmate’s?
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    Consider the function \(f(x,y) = x^{2} - 2xy + y\text{.}\)
    🔗

    (a)

    Find the gradient \(\nabla f(x,y)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    If you are at the point \((1,1)\text{,}\) what is the gradient there?
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Suppose \(f(x,y)\) is measuring the elevation of a landscape in feet. In what direction should you move from the point \((1,1)\) to move most steeply uphill? In what direction should you move to stay at the same elevation?
    🔗
    🔗

    (d)

    Suppose your motion in the plane is given by the function \(r(t) = (t^{2}, t^{3})\text{.}\) What is your location at time \(t=1\) and what is your velocity?
    🔗
    🔗

    (e)

    If \(t\) is measured in seconds, at what rate, in feet per second, are you moving uphill at \(t=1\text{?}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    4.

    Sketch the field \(F(x,y) = (y^{2}, 2x)\) at the nine points \((x,y)\) where \(x\) and \(y\) take on the values \(-1\text{,}\) \(0\text{,}\) and \(1\text{.}\) (You don’t necessarily need to draw the vectors to scale.)
