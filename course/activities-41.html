    Worksheet 40: Principal values of integrals
    

    1.

    Consider the integral
    \begin{equation*} \int_{-\infty}^\infty\frac1{1+x^3}~dx, \end{equation*}
    where the integrand has a singularity at \(x=-1\text{.}\) Find the principal value of this integral by integrating around a small loop going above the singularity.
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    In quantum mechanics, determining the intensity of scattered particles as a function of the cross section \(\sigma\) leads to an integral of the form
    \begin{equation*} I(\sigma) = \int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} \frac{x \sin x}{x^2- \sigma^2} ~dx, \end{equation*}
    where \(\sigma\) is real and positive. Using the theory of residues, evaluate the integral \(I(\sigma)\text{,}\) interpreted as the principal value.
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    Evaluate
    \begin{equation*} \int_{-\infty}^\infty \frac{1}{x^4+5x^2+4}~dx. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗

    4.

    Find the principal value of the integral
    \begin{equation*} \int_{-\infty}^\infty\frac{e^{ix}}{x}~dx \end{equation*}
    and use your result to evaluate
    \begin{equation*} \int_{-\infty}^\infty\frac{\sin x}{x}~dx. \end{equation*}
    🔗
