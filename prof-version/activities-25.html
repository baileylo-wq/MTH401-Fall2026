    Worksheet 2.14. Bessel functions of the first kind
    

    MTH 401

    Definition 31.

    Recall that Bessel’s equation of order \(n\) is
    \begin{equation*} x^2 y'' + xy'+(x^2-n^2)y = 0. \end{equation*}
    Solutions are found using the Method of Frobenius, that is assuming it has the form \({y(x)=a_0 x^p + a_1 x^{p+1}+a_2x^{p+2}+\cdots}\) for some unknown \(p\) and solving for the coefficients.
    🔗
    For Bessel’s functions of the first kind we take \(p = n\text{.}\) We find that all odd coefficients are zero (\({a_1 = a_3 = a_5 = \ldots = 0}\)), the coefficient \(a_0\) is a free variable, and the other even coefficients are
    \begin{equation*} a_{2m} = \frac{-a_{2(m-1)}}{4m(n+m)} \end{equation*}
    for \(m=1, 2, 3, \ldots\text{.}\) The solution to the order \(n\) equation is denoted \(J_n(x)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    1.

    Find a solution to Bessel’s equation of order 1
    \begin{equation*} x^2 y'' + xy' + (x^2 - 1^2) y = 0 \end{equation*}
    that is bounded at \(x=0\text{.}\) Use Mathematica to draw a graph of it.
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    We are not limited to integers. Find a solution to Bessel’s equation of order 3/2
    \begin{equation*} x^2 y'' + xy' + \left(x^2 - \left(\frac32\right)^2\right) y = 0 \end{equation*}
    that is bounded at \(x=0\text{.}\) Use Mathematica to draw a graph of it.
    🔗
    🔗
    Fun facts!
    • Bessel functions of integer order show up when solving problems in cylindrical coordinate systems, and but half-integer orders of Bessel functions (\(n=m+\frac12\)) appear in spherical problems.
      🔗
      🔗
    • When solving Bessel’s equation of order \(n\text{,}\) if we take \(p=-n\) then we will find Bessel’s functions of the second kind, which we denote \(Y_n(x)\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    • Hankel functions are complex functions that come from combining Bessel functions of the first and second kind, so \(H_n^{(1)} = J_n+iY_n\) are Hankel functions of the first kind, and \(H_n^{(2)} = J_n-iY_n\) are of the second kind. (We won’t get into these...)
      🔗
      🔗
